What did Ratcliffe bet on?

INEOS paid $33 a share for a quarter of Manchester United. This model plays out the next ten seasons thousands of times. League finish drives prize money, European qualification, sponsor clauses and wage clauses, and each future becomes a set of cash flows. Move the sliders to test your own view of the price.

The bet: how good do United need to be?

Each point re-runs the simulation with United's underlying strength set to a different level, from a mid-table side to a perennial champion, and plots the buyer's median return. The return comes from paying the price, collecting ten years of cash flow and selling at the exit multiple. The dashed line is the cost of capital.

One season's money by European competition

Method. Elo ratings on every Premier League match since 2010-11 give a win/draw/loss link. A Kalman-filtered state-space model (a drifting club level plus fading form) evolves every club's strength. Each path plays full seasons, applies English qualification rules and simulates European knockout rounds. Revenue is mapped from results: PL merit payments, UEFA prize money, European gates, the kit-deal penalty after two seasons outside the Champions League, and the Champions League wage clause. The base year is reproduced exactly. FCFF = adjusted EBITDA − tax − net player spend − capex.

Code and full report: the manu_valuation package and docs/valuation/report.md in the De-Zerbi-research repository. Deal inputs (share count, net debt, transfer payables), sponsor and wage clause sizes are estimates. See manu_valuation/config.py.
